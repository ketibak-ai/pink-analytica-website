I Am Afraid to Be Alarmist, But I Think It's Time to Sound the Alarms

October 9, 2026

I increasingly feel that society is heading down a path of self-destruction, not necessarily through one catastrophic event, but through the gradual erosion of the very things that allow us to function as a society.

I recently read a New York Times interview about the concept of post-literacy, and it struck me how closely these ideas connect. We are losing not only the habit of reading deeply, but also the ability to engage deeply with ideas and with one another.

We increasingly live in information ecosystems that reinforce what we already believe. We listen to voices that validate our opinions rather than challenge them. Where are the thoughtful, respectful debates in which people genuinely listen, question their own assumptions, and remain open to changing their minds?

Disagreement used to be an opportunity to think more deeply. Now, too often, it becomes a reason to dismiss, attack, or disconnect from one another.

My biggest concern is that we are losing the very skills that make a functioning society possible: critical thinking, intellectual curiosity, empathy, and the ability to have difficult conversations without turning each other into enemies.

A society that loses its capacity for thoughtful dialogue may eventually lose its capacity to correct itself. And that is what worries me most.
