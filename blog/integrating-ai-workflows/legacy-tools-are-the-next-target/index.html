Legacy Tools Are the Next Target

October 8, 2026

An old legacy tool, in any industry or institution, is no longer protected — no matter how old it is or how long it has been embedded in a company's processes. Millions of lines of code have been developed to build these tools, and year after year, layer after layer, things have been added. It's the complexity and the many years of legacy that have kept competitors at bay.

However, with AI agents and AI in general, things have changed dramatically. Legacy software could be next. Existing projects — the messy stuff nobody wants to touch because of the complexity — not anymore. Agents, built properly, can deconstruct and rebuild, with human interaction and knowledge, fast, and without losing context. These shifts can happen quickly once a small prototype is built. And then the dynamic starts to shift.

  • Legacy drag gone. Better to start from scratch while rebuilding and rewiring. Cleaner code, cleaner workflows, cleaner processes, cleaner documentation. You improve as you go, but at the same time build and maintain everything. Humans monitor and instruct, but agents execute.
  • Velocity gains. This time the teams will be quick and not haggle over when and why. The agent will do a lot of the grunt work. Humans are directing and guiding so things do not go amiss. They are the conductors and have the knowledge depth to build the rational architecture.
  • Revamp the old. New features and new options will be able to be added or taken out as needed. As the demands and needs shift, so will the tools adapt.

As the cost of software becomes cheaper and AI tools become more accurate, the ability to change is becoming the new currency. Building something to last is no longer a sign that you're good. Adaptability and change are in.
